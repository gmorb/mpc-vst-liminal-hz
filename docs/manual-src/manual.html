Chapter 1

Welcome

Real rooms, echoes, impossible spaces and textures, from impulse responses, on the MPC itself.

Liminal Hz is a convolution reverb that runs inside your Akai MPC or Force as a native insert effect, with its own touchscreen page. Load the recording of a real room, a hall, a stairwell, a tape echo or something stranger, and your sound plays as if it were inside that space. Then reshape it (Decay, Stretch, Reverse, trim, fades, Feedback), keep it out of the groove with Duck, lock its pre-delay to your tempo with Sync, and keep the result as one of My Presets.

It comes with 19 factory IRs in four kinds (Spaces, Echoes, Strange and Textures), browses impulse responses you put on your cards, and downloads more from TONE3000 straight to the MPC, using your phone as the keyboard and screen. It's a companion to Liminal Amp: put Liminal Hz after your amp to place it in a room.

What is an impulse response?

Clap once in a big empty hall and listen: the clap is followed by a cloud of reflections that fades away. Record that cloud and you've captured how the hall responds to a short, sharp sound. That recording is an impulse response (IR). It holds everything about the space: how long it rings, which frequencies die first, how early the first walls answer, how wide it sounds.

Convolution plays any sound through that recording, as if every moment of your sound were its own clap in the hall. It isn't a simulation of a room: it is the room. An IR doesn't have to be a room, either: tape echoes, springs, a struck pipe, a chord or a burst of noise all make useful IRs, which is where the Echoes, Strange and Textures sets come in.

Not a modelled reverb. An algorithmic reverb builds a space from knobs like "room size". Liminal Hz starts from a real response: you change the space by choosing an IR, then sculpt it.

Option A: MPC OS 3.10.1 and later NEW

MPC OS 3.10.1 loads third-party plugins by itself, with no install script and no root access. One download works on every unit, Gen 1 and Gen 2.

  1. On the MPC, open Preferences › Enhancements (Beta) and turn on Enable Enhancements and External Plugin Support. Read the warning: Akai doesn't test or support third-party plugins.
  2. Unzip Liminal-Hz-{{version}}-MPC-OS-3.10.1.zip on your computer.
  3. At the top level of a USB drive, SD card or the internal drive, make a folder named exactly VST2 Plugins (if it isn't there), and copy the Gm0rb - VST - Liminal Hz folder into it.
  4. Put the drive in the MPC. Add an insert effect to a track: Liminal Hz is under External Plugins.

Coming from an older install? Copy your presets folder from the old plugin folder into the new one, then remove the old install so Liminal Hz isn't listed twice. Your reverbs folders on your drives are found as before. Projects saved with the older install haven't been confirmed to reconnect to the new one: before you switch, save the sound in each project you care about as a My Preset. If a project opens without the plugin, add Liminal Hz again and load that preset.

Earlier MPC OS 3.x

These routes are unofficial and need access to your unit beyond what stock MPC OS offers: SSH on Gen 1 (for example through MockbaMod firmware), a root shell on Gen 2. Save your project and back up anything you care about first: installing stops and restarts MPC and edits MPC's settings file (the installer backs it up).

Option B: the MPC OS Plugin Catalog

If you use the MPC OS Plugin Catalog (its store command or desktop installer), install Liminal Hz from there. It picks the right build for your unit: armv7 on Gen 1, aarch64 on Gen 2.

Option C: the installer in the release zip

The -mpc-armv7.zip (Gen 1) and -mpc-aarch64.zip (Gen 2) contain the same kind of installer. It checks the unit (and refuses the wrong generation's zip), stops MPC, copies the plugin folder, backs up MPC.settings, registers the plugin and starts MPC again.

scp -r Liminal-Hz-{{version}} root@<unit-ip>:/tmp/
ssh root@<unit-ip> sh /tmp/Liminal-Hz-{{version}}/install.sh

Add -t <folder> to install into another Synths folder, such as one on a card (on some Gen 2 units the default internal location isn't writable: use -t /media/<card>/Synths). -y skips the confirmation. When MPC is back, Liminal Hz is in the plugin browser under Gm0rb.

Option D: Force VST distribution folder (Gen 1)

For Gen 1 units set up with the Force VST plugin distribution and its vstscanner: copy the Gm0rb - VST - Liminal Hz folder from Liminal-Hz-for-MPC-OS-{{version}}.zip into the Synths folder on your card, then run vstscanner. MPC restarts and Liminal Hz appears under Gm0rb.

Updating and removing

Updating: copy the new folder over the old one (Option A or D), or run the new installer (C), or update from the Catalog (B). Your reverbs and presets folders are kept. Then remove and re-add the plugin on your tracks so MPC picks up the new parameter list, and if the page still looks like the old version, restart MPC: it keeps plugin pages in memory.

Removing: delete the folder from VST2 Plugins (Option A), run uninstall.sh from the release zip (C), or remove it in the Catalog (B). Files you added yourself are kept. Projects that used the plugin open without it.

Chapter 3

Quick start

Ten steps from an empty insert slot to a sound of your own.

  1. Insert it. On a track (or a submix or return), open the insert effects, choose Liminal Hz (under External Plugins on MPC OS 3.10.1, under Gm0rb otherwise) and open its page. It starts as Init: no IR loaded, your sound passes straight through.
  2. Pick a factory preset. Tap the PRESET field at the top left. A menu of 19 presets opens in four columns. Try Fluorescent Hall. (MPC's own preset menu, at the top of the screen, has the same presets grouped by use.)
  3. Play. Hit a pad or play your instrument. Watch the glow drift along the shape display: that's where your sound is in the space right now.
  4. Set the amount. Turn Mix, the big knob at the bottom left. 20 to 35% sits a sound in a room; 100% is all room (use 100% on a send/return).
  5. Try other spaces. Tap ◀ ▶ beside the IR name to step through IRs, or IR LIST to see six at a time.
  6. Make it bigger or smaller. Turn Decay (in the time group). Above 100% the room rings longer without changing its pitch or colour.
  7. Give it room to breathe. Add 20 to 40 ms of Pre-delay so the dry attack lands before the reverb arrives, or switch on Sync and pick a note value.
  8. Keep the groove clear. Turn up Duck (in the out group): the reverb steps back while you play and blooms in the gaps. Then raise Low Cut to 150 to 300 Hz to keep the low end tight.
  9. Keep it. Tap MY PRESETS, then SAVE. It's saved under the IR's name.
  10. Name it properly (optional). In the My Presets list tap NAME ON PHONE, open the address it shows on your phone, and type a name.

Chapter 5

Choosing a space

Presets set everything at once. Factory IRs change only the space. Your own IRs start from Init.

Factory presets

Tap the PRESET field (top left) to open the preset menu: four columns, plus Init. Picking a preset loads its factory IR together with settings that suit it: Mix, Pre-delay, cuts, Width, Output, fades and Feedback. Change anything you like afterwards; the preset is only a starting point.

  • Echoes are distinct repeats: slapback, flutter between close walls, tape delay, ping-pong across the stereo field, a lo-fi intercom.

Picking a factory preset there is the same as picking it in the plugin. The list of your IRs is read when the plugin loads, so IRs you add or delete show up the next time you load it or reopen a project. Re-selecting the preset in use changes nothing, and a project always reopens with its own sound, whatever the menu lists then.

Factory and My IRs

The FACTORY / MY IRS switch chooses which list ◀ ▶, the pack buttons and IR LIST step through. Factory is the 19 built-in IRs. My IRs is every IR in your reverbs folders, including TONE3000 downloads. Switching lists never changes what's playing; it only changes what you browse.

  • A factory IR keeps your knob settings. Step through the factory IRs and only the space changes.
  • One of your own IRs starts from Init NEW. Picking one (with the arrows, the packs, the IR List, MPC's menu or a TONE3000 download) puts the knobs back to Init's, whether you came from a factory preset or another of your IRs: a sound's settings belong to its IR. Shape it from there; picking the same IR again keeps your changes. To keep a sound, save it as a My Preset.

Stepping through IRs

◀ ▶ beside the IR name load the previous or next IR in the list. The new IR crossfades in (about 93 ms), so you can step while playing without clicks. PREV PACK / NEXT PACK jump to the first IR of the previous or next pack. A pack is a subfolder (for factory IRs: Spaces, Echoes, Strange, Textures; for TONE3000: one downloaded tone). Below the name: the pack and a details line, for example "IR 2 of 5 · Local · Stereo". For TONE3000 IRs the details show the gear type and creator, and the T3K mark appears.

The IR List

For long lists, tap IR LIST. The list you're in covers the browser, six to a page, as "pack · name". The one playing is outlined.

  • Tap a row to load that IR. The list stays open, so you can audition one after another.
  • PREV / NEXT turn the pages, one page per tap.
  • FACTORY / MY IRS switch lists without closing.
  • CLOSE puts the browser back. Opening a project closes it too.

RESCAN

Liminal Hz finds your IRs when it starts. After you add, rename or remove files on a card (or edit preset files from a computer), tap RESCAN. It also remembers where it found your reverbs folders, so starting up stays fast even on a full card (chapter 10).

DELETE

DELETE removes the IR in use from your card, permanently, and moves on to the next IR. It needs two taps: the first only asks (the line shows a confirmation request); tap again within 4 seconds to delete. The second tap must come at least a third of a second after the first, so one touch can't do both. Factory IRs can't be deleted.

There's no undo. A deleted IR is gone from the card. If it came from TONE3000, you can download it again.

Chapter 6

The controls

Two kinds of control: the ones that mix the reverb with your sound, and the ones that reshape the IR itself.

The space, tone and out groups act on the sound in real time. The trim, fade and time groups and Reverse reshape the IR: each change is prepared in the background and crossfaded in, so moving them never costs the audio anything, but the result arrives a moment later (longer IRs take longer to prepare, especially with Decay).

space

Mix 0–100% · default 30%

How much of the reverb you hear. At 0% only the dry sound; at 100% only the reverb. As Mix rises, the dry signal is turned down by the same amount, so the overall level stays roughly even. Use 100% when Liminal Hz sits on a send/return, and 15 to 40% as an insert. Mix glides to new values, so turning it never zips or clicks.

Pre-delay 0–250 ms · default 0 ms

A gap before the reverb starts. It keeps attacks clear (the dry hit lands, then the room answers) and makes a space feel bigger. 10 to 30 ms for vocals and drums; 60 to 120 ms for a dramatic, separated tail. Pre-delay glides, so it can be moved while playing.

Sync Off / On · default Off NEW

The switch beside Pre-delay puts it in time with your project. With Sync on, the Pre-delay knob steps through note values at the project tempo:

0 1/128 1/64 1/32 1/16 T 1/16 1/16 D 1/8 T 1/8 1/8 D 1/4

T is a triplet and D is dotted. The tempo is read from MPC continuously, so the delay follows tempo changes, gliding like a knob move. A synced pre-delay can reach 2 seconds (a 1/4 note at 30 BPM). Try 1/16 on drums for a room that answers on the grid, or 1/8 D with a little Feedback for rhythmic echoes.

Feedback 0–100% · default 0%

Feeds the reverb's output back into itself, so every reflection is reflected again: longer, building tails.

  • Gentle at first: the knob is eased, so the first half builds quickly and the top half is fine control. Around 50% tails last about 2 to 4 times as long.
  • From about 70%: a long ring that holds at roughly the level you play at and fades away over 30 to 40 seconds once you stop.
  • Each repeat is a little darker (a 4 kHz damping filter in the loop), like a tape echo, so long feedback turns into a warm wash rather than a whistle.
  • It can't run away. The loop is scaled to each IR's spectrum and watched by a guard that turns the fed-back signal down whenever the reverb gets louder than your recent input.

tone

Low Cut 20–1000 Hz · default 80 Hz and High Cut 1–20 kHz · default 12 kHz

Filters on the reverb only (the dry sound is never filtered). Each turns on a logarithmic scale, so every octave gets the same turn of the knob: the middle of Low Cut is about 141 Hz and the middle of High Cut about 4.5 kHz. Both are 12 dB per octave. Raise Low Cut to keep kick drums, bass and low guitar notes from turning the room to mud (150 to 300 Hz for most mixes); lower High Cut for warmth, distance or an old-recording feel.

Width 0–200% · default 100%

The stereo width of the reverb. 100% is as recorded; 0% is mono (the reverb sits in the centre with your sound); above 100% the sides are pushed out further, up to 200%. Width glides too.

out

Duck 0–100% · default 0% NEW

Turns the reverb down while you play and lets it bloom in the gaps, so big rooms on snares and vocals don't muddy the groove. At 100% the reverb drops by up to 24 dB under a loud input and comes back within about a quarter of a second after it stops. The attack and release are set for you: one knob is all there is.

  • It only turns down what you hear, after the feedback loop, so Feedback's tails carry on underneath and return when you stop.
  • At 0% the sound is exactly as without it.
  • Try 40 to 70% on a vocal with a long hall: words stay clear and the hall fills the pauses.

Output −24 to +12 dB · default 0 dB

The level of everything leaving the plugin, dry and wet together. IRs are levelled to similar loudness when they load, and the factory presets are levelled to sit close to your dry sound, so you rarely need it except to match the bypassed level. It glides to new values.

trim

Start 0–90% · default 0%

Skips the beginning of the IR. Cutting off the first few percent removes the direct sound and early reflections, leaving only the diffuse tail: the room without its walls. Higher values make strange, detached ambiences.

End 5–100% · default 100%

Where the IR stops, as a share of its length. The quickest way to make a long space shorter, and the cheapest: a shorter IR uses less CPU. (In the parameter list this is called Length.) Start and End always keep at least 128 samples of IR, so the space never vanishes entirely.

fade

Fade In 0–50% · default 0%

Fades the kept part of the IR in, as a share of its length. The reverb swells up instead of starting at once: soft, bowed, ghostly. Combine with Pre-delay for a reverb that blooms behind the note.

Fade Out 0–50% · default 10%

Fades the end of the IR out. Even at 0% a tiny fade is applied, so a trimmed IR never ends with a click. Raise it for a smoother, more natural decay on IRs that end abruptly.

time

Stretch 50–200% · default 100%

Resamples the IR like a tape speed change. Above 100% the space gets longer and lower (a 200% room is twice as long and an octave down); below 100% it gets shorter and higher. Great for turning a room into a cave, or a hall into a small, bright box. With LONG IR on and on Gen 2 the resampling is band-limited, so even 50% stays clean.

Decay 50–300% · default 100%

How long the room rings, without changing its pitch or its colour. Decay measures how quickly each band of frequencies (in third-octaves) dies away, then makes each band fade faster or slower by the same factor. When you lengthen a room past the end of its recording, each band is continued "in kind" with matching noise, so the tail goes on naturally rather than stopping.

  • 100% plays the IR exactly as recorded.
  • A small dark room stays small and dark, just longer: Backrooms at 300% rings about 2.5 times as long.
  • Longer decays come out slightly louder, as a bigger room would (about +3 dB at 300%).
  • On Gen 1 the result is capped at 5 seconds, so long IRs gain less there. With LONG IR on, and on Gen 2, the cap is 20 seconds: a 5-second room at 300% rings its full 15 seconds or so.

Decay or Stretch? Stretch changes time and pitch together, like tape. Decay changes only how long the tail lasts. To make a room "bigger" naturally, use Decay; to make it alien, use Stretch.

Reverse

Plays the IR backwards. The reverb swells up into each note instead of trailing after it: the classic reverse-reverb effect, live. Reverse is applied after the length limit and before Start and End, so Start and End then trim the reversed IR. Try it with a short End (30 to 50%) for a tight pre-swell, or with Pre-delay for a gap between the swell and the note.

LONG IR

The on/off switch in the lower right, under Reverse, labelled Long IR. On Gen 1 it switches on Gen 2's IR quality:

  • IRs up to 20 seconds instead of 5, so long halls, drones and long echo IRs play whole, and Decay can stretch a room past 5 seconds.
  • Band-limited resampling for IRs recorded at other sample rates and for Stretch: no dulled top octave, no alias tones.
  • Finer Decay analysis: twice the frequency detail, so the bass is measured accurately.

One tap turns it on and one turns it off, like Reverse. While it's on, the line under it reads "On · CPU heavy · 20 s"; while it's off, "Off · IRs up to 5 s". Turning it on or off prepares the current IR again and crossfades it in, so you can switch while playing. Long IRs use more of the second core and more memory, and take longer to prepare after each change. If the details line shows Tail late, turn LONG IR off or shorten the IR with End.

It's a setting for your unit, not part of a sound: a project remembers it, but presets, Init and My Presets leave it as it is, and it can't be automated. On Gen 2 it's always on and the line reads "Gen 2 · always on".

The order things happen in

When the IR is prepared, the steps run in this order: resample to the MPC's rate with Stretch → Decay → cut to the length limit → Reverse → Start and End → Fade In and Fade Out → level matching. Knowing the order explains a few things: Start trims the reversed IR when Reverse is on, and the fades always apply to what's left after trimming.

Chapter 7

Reading the shape display

The right side of the page shows what the IR sounds like, after all your shaping.

The sonics line

A one-line description in words, for example "decay 2.8 s · airy · darkens · wide · diffuse". It updates whenever the IR or its shaping changes.

The painting

The IR drawn as a misty trace in twelve columns across its length. Height is the level over time, on a decibel scale reaching down to about −66 dB, so a reverb's whole audible decay is visible. Colour is its tone at that moment: pale ice is airy, with lots of high frequencies; bone is balanced and warm; dusky violet is dark, the highs having died away. Most real spaces go from pale to bone to violet as they fade. Move End, Fade Out or Decay and the painting follows.

The glow

After each note, a glow drifts along under the painting, showing where your sound is in the space right now, pre-delay included. It's the playhead of the reverb: when it reaches the right-hand end, the tail of that note has finished. It listens for new notes in the audio you play (hits, plucks, and pads with slow attacks; a held, beating chord or tremolo doesn't keep restarting it), and also follows MIDI note-ons whenever the MPC sends MIDI to the plugin.

Chapter 8

My Presets

Set the plugin the way you like it, then keep it.

Tap MY PRESETS (beside the Preset field). Your presets cover the browser, four to a page, with PREV / NEXT / CLOSE as in the IR List.

Saving

SAVE keeps the current sound: every knob (Duck and Sync included) and the IR in use, factory or your own. It's named after the IR ("Fluorescent Hall", then "Fluorescent Hall 2" and so on) and becomes the selected preset.

Loading

Tap a row to load that preset: its settings and its IR, exactly as saved. The one in use is outlined, the Preset field shows "My: <name>", and a project saved now remembers which preset it was. Loading a My Preset never goes back to Init, even when its IR is one of yours.

  • If the preset's IR has moved (another folder, another card), Liminal Hz finds it again by its name.
  • If the IR is gone, the settings still load and the line says the IR wasn't found ("Not found: …").

Deleting

DELETE removes the selected preset. Like IR delete, it asks first: tap twice, the second tap at least a third of a second after the first and within 4 seconds. The file is deleted permanently.

Naming on your phone

The MPC's screen has no keyboard, so names are typed on your phone. Tap NAME ON PHONE: the line shows an address such as 192.168.1.20:8191/presets. Open it in your phone's browser (same Wi-Fi as the MPC) to save the current sound under a typed name, or to load, rename and delete presets. This page needs no internet connection and no account. Chapter 9 has more on the phone pages.

The files

Each preset is a small text file in the plugin's presets/ folder, named <name>.lhzp. They're kept when you update the plugin. You can rename, copy, back up or delete them from a computer; tap RESCAN afterwards and the list follows. Copy them to another unit (Gen 1 or Gen 2) to take your sounds with you, or into the new folder when you move to the MPC OS 3.10.1 install.

Chapter 9

Your phone: TONE3000 and preset names

The plugin serves two small pages to a phone or computer on the same Wi-Fi. No app to install, no password.

Opening the pages

  1. Tap BROWSE in the TONE3000 strip. The status line shows an address, for example Open 192.168.1.20:8191.
  2. On a phone on the same Wi-Fi as the MPC, open http:// followed by that address.

The pages close themselves after 15 idle minutes (the line then says "Page closed"). Tap BROWSE again to reopen.

Downloading IRs from TONE3000

TONE3000 is a large online library of captures of real spaces and gear, made by a community of musicians. To get an IR from it:

  1. On the phone's home page, tap Continue: Space, Outboard, Pedal and Experimental IRs.
  2. Sign in to TONE3000 (on your phone) and pick a tone from the Space, Outboard, Pedal or Experimental categories.
  3. It downloads straight to the MPC, into reverbs/TONE3000/<tone>/ in the plugin's folder. The status line on the MPC shows the progress ("Downloading 1/3") and the new IR is selected when it's done, starting from Init's settings.
  • Only IRs are offered, and only .wav files are downloaded. A tone that isn't an IR is refused.
  • A TONE3000 tone becomes one pack; its variants (different mics, positions or lengths) are separate IRs within it, which ◀ ▶ step through.
  • The MPC itself needs internet access for the download.

The My Presets page

Reached from NAME ON PHONE (the address ends in /presets) or the My Presets: name & save button on the home page. It works without internet. Save the current sound under a typed name (tick "replace it if the name exists" to overwrite), or load, rename and delete any preset. The one in use is marked "selected".

Chapter 10

Your own IRs

Any WAV impulse response works: recorded rooms, commercial IR packs, ones you make yourself.

Where to put them

Put .wav files in any folder named reverbs (any capitalisation): inside the plugin's own folder, on any card or drive, or on the internal drive, up to four folders deep. Subfolders become packs. Then tap RESCAN and switch to MY IRS. Example: /media/<card>/reverbs/Churches/Nave.wav and …/reverbs/Churches/Crypt.wav form a pack called "Churches". They also appear in MPC's preset menu as "My IR: …" the next time the plugin loads.

Liminal Hz remembers where it found your reverbs folders in a small hidden file, .reverbs-index, next to the plugin. At start-up it loads the saved IR at once and searches for changes in the background. If you add a reverbs folder on a new card, tap RESCAN once and it's found and remembered.

Formats

PropertySupported
TypeWAV, including the "extensible" WAVs many DAWs write
Sample format16, 24 and 32-bit integer PCM; 32 and 64-bit float
Sample rateAny. IRs are converted to the MPC's rate when they load (band-limited sinc with LONG IR on, and on Gen 2)
ChannelsMono or stereo. With more than two channels, the first two are used

Length usedUp to 5 s on Gen 1, 20 s with LONG IR on and on Gen 2. Longer IRs are cut to the limit and faded out; End shortens them further

Mono and stereo IRs

Stereo IRs (most reverb IRs) take the mono sum of your input and give their own left and right: that's how spaces are recorded, with a source in the room and two microphones. Mono IRs process your left and right channels separately with the same IR, keeping your stereo image. Use Width to spread them. Every IR is levelled to the same energy when it loads, so quiet and loud files come out at similar loudness: you don't need to normalise them.

Making your own

  • Sine sweep: play a sweep through a space or a piece of gear, record it, and deconvolve it with an IR tool. The cleanest IRs.
  • Balloon pop or clap: record one sharp sound in a space. Rougher, but quick and characterful.
  • Anything short: a drum hit, a vocal phrase, a chord from your MPC, a reversed cymbal. Your sound takes on its envelope and colour: this is where the Strange and Textures presets come from.

Trim any silence before the first sound in an IR file (or use Start), or the reverb arrives late.

How they fall on your unit's Q-Link banks depends on the unit. Mix, Pre-delay, Feedback, Duck, the cuts, Width and Output respond instantly and smoothly. The IR-shaping controls (Decay, Stretch, Start, End, the fades, Reverse) prepare a new IR in the background on each change and crossfade it in, so sweeping them quickly gives a stepped, morphing effect rather than a smooth sweep.

Automation

All the sound controls can be recorded as automation. The display parts of the page (the painting, the glow, the sonics line) and LONG IR are marked as not automatable, so MPC never records them.

MIDI

Liminal Hz is an effect, so MIDI only matters for the glow: each note-on the MPC sends restarts the playhead. Sync takes the tempo from the project.

What a project remembers

  • Every parameter value, Duck and Sync included.
  • The IR in use, by its path, its path relative to the reverbs folder, and its file name. If you've moved the IR to another folder or card, it's found again.
  • Which My Preset was selected, if any.
  • Whether LONG IR was on.

A project opens on the page itself, with no list left open, and always with its own sound: when MPC re-selects a preset from its menu as the project loads, Liminal Hz keeps the project's settings. If the IR can't be found, the project opens with its settings and the line says "Not found: <file>": put the file back in a reverbs folder and tap RESCAN. Projects move between Gen 1 and Gen 2.

Projects from 1.3 and earlier keep their sound. Duck and Sync arrive off.

Chapter 12

Sound design recipes

Starting points. Every IR is different, so treat the numbers as a place to begin, then listen.

Tight drum room

Start from Backrooms · Mix 15–25% · Pre-delay 0–5 ms · Low Cut 200 Hz · High Cut 8 kHz · End 40–60%

Puts dry samples in a real-sounding room without washing them out. A short End keeps it tight; Low Cut stops the kick from booming.

Big snare that stays out of the way NEW

Start from Empty Mall or Fluorescent Hall · Mix 30–45% · Decay 150–200% · Duck 60–80% · Low Cut 250 Hz

The room blooms after each hit and ducks under the next one, so a huge space doesn't blur the groove.

Vocal hall with clarity

Start from Fluorescent Hall · Mix 20–30% · Pre-delay 30–60 ms · Duck 40–70% · Low Cut 250 Hz · High Cut 9 kHz

Pre-delay lets every consonant land before the hall answers, and Duck keeps the words clear while the hall fills the pauses.

Room on the grid NEW

Start from Parking Slap · Sync on · Pre-delay 1/16 or 1/8 D · Mix 25–35% · Feedback 20–40%

The room answers in time with the beat. Dotted values give a skipping, swung echo; Feedback turns it into a rhythmic trail.

Guitar amp in a room (after Liminal Amp)

Start from Pool Rooms or Empty Mall · Mix 15–25% · Pre-delay 10–20 ms · Low Cut 150 Hz · Width 130%

Put Liminal Hz after Liminal Amp on the same track. A little room turns a close-miked amp into a player in a space; Width spreads a mono amp.

Dub delay trail

Start from Tape Corridor or Dream Ping-Pong · Mix 30–45% · Feedback 50–75% · Low Cut 300 Hz · High Cut 4–6 kHz

Feedback stretches the repeats into a long, darkening trail. Ride Mix or Feedback on a Q-Link for throws: turn it up on the last word of a phrase.

Reverse swell into a snare

Start from Reverse Bloom, or any hall with Reverse on · End 30–50% · Mix 40–60% · Pre-delay 0 ms

The reverb rises into each hit. Use it on a duplicate of the snare on its own track and mute the dry copy for a pure swell.

Drums that play a chord NEW

Start from Tuned Chord · Mix 10–20% · Low Cut 150 Hz · Width 100%

Every hit rings the chord. Keep Mix low: the chord is resonant and builds quickly. Try it on hats and percussion too.

Metal and grains NEW

Start from Struck Pipe or Grain Cloud · Stretch 150–200% · Decay 200–250% · Mix 20–40%

Stretch drops the pipe into a cavern of metal or slows the grains into a drifting cloud. Great on vocal chops and risers.

Ambient pad wash

Start from Shimmer Fog or Glass Void · Mix 50–100% · Decay 200–300% · Fade In 10–25% · Feedback 30–50% · Width 160–200%

Fade In makes the reverb bloom behind each chord; long Decay and a little Feedback hold it in the air. With LONG IR on (or on Gen 2), Decay 300% on a long IR can ring for 15 seconds or more.

Cave, from any room

Any Spaces IR · Stretch 160–200% · Decay 150% · High Cut 5 kHz · Pre-delay 60 ms

Stretch makes a room longer and lower: an octave down at 200%. A low High Cut and a long Pre-delay add distance and damp stone.

Drone machine

Start from the Liminal Hz preset · Feedback 75–90% · Mix 60–100% · Start 10–20%

Play a short note and the space keeps ringing at about the level you played, then fades over 30 to 40 seconds when you stop. Start removes the attack so only the ringing body is left.

Lo-fi intercom

Start from Intercom Echo · Low Cut 400–600 Hz · High Cut 3–4 kHz · Width 0% · Mix 40–70%

A narrow band and mono width turn any part into a voice from another room.

Send/return reverb for a whole beat

Insert on a return · Mix 100% · Low Cut 250–400 Hz · Pre-delay 20 ms

One instance shared by many tracks puts them all in the same space (and costs CPU once). Set each track's send level for how far back it sits.

Chapter 13

Performance and how it works

Zero latency, and long IRs that stay cheap.

Zero latency

Liminal Hz adds no delay to your sound. The first part of every IR runs directly in MPC's own 128-sample audio blocks, so the reverb starts at the same moment as the dry sound.

How long IRs stay cheap

A 5-second stereo IR has 220,500 points per channel. Convolving it the simple way in MPC's tiny audio blocks would cost far more than a whole block of time. Liminal Hz splits each IR into parts:

  • The head, the first 4,096 samples (93 ms): processed on MPC's audio thread in 128-sample blocks, the same small cost every block.
  • The tail, the rest: processed in larger blocks on a separate worker thread, on another processor core. Each block is computed a whole block ahead of when it's needed.
  • The far tail. For IRs longer than about 0.74 seconds, everything past that point goes to a third stage with much larger blocks (16,384 samples) on its own worker thread. That makes long IRs much cheaper, and keeps a 20-second IR efficient.

The audio thread's cost doesn't grow with IR length on either generation: it always runs the same 93 ms head. The result is exactly the same as direct convolution, to float precision.

CPU use

Measured on an Akai Force with version 1.2.0, a 5-second stereo IR:

Gen 1 (Akai Force)Measured
MPC's audio thread, averageabout 3.4% of a block
MPC's audio thread, worst blockabout 7%
Worker thread (on another core)about 8% of a core
Late tail blocks0

Since 1.3 the far-tail stage and the processor's NEON vector maths are used on every unit, so the worker's load is lower than these figures; the audio thread's cost is unchanged. A mono IR costs about half. Shorter IRs and lower End settings cost less on the worker core. Gen 2 has far more headroom.

Saving CPU: use one instance on a return instead of one per track, trim long IRs with End to what you actually hear, and use a mono IR where width doesn't matter.

LONG IR and Gen 2

LONG IR (always on on Gen 2) allows 20-second IRs and full-length Decay, resamples IRs with a band-limited sinc filter (a 48 kHz IR keeps its top octave, and Stretch below 100% no longer makes alias tones), and analyses Decay with twice the frequency detail. Its cost is mostly in preparing the IR, off the audio thread: loading a long IR at another sample rate, Stretch and Decay take longer; Start, End, the fades and Reverse stay quick.

Common problems

Liminal Hz isn't under External Plugins (MPC OS 3.10.1)

  • Check Preferences › Enhancements (Beta): both Enable Enhancements and External Plugin Support must be on.
  • The folder must be named exactly VST2 Plugins, at the top level of the drive, and the plugin's folder must sit directly inside it (not one folder deeper).
  • Make sure you copied the folder from the -MPC-OS-3.10.1.zip: the other zips don't have the files this route needs.

Liminal Hz is listed twice

An older install (Plugin Catalog, installer or Synths folder) is still there next to the VST2 Plugins copy. Copy your presets folder across, then remove the older install.

Liminal Hz isn't in the plugin list (earlier MPC OS)

  • Make sure you installed the right zip for your generation: armv7 for Gen 1, aarch64 for Gen 2. A 32-bit plugin can't load on Gen 2, and the reverse. The installer refuses the wrong one.
  • Look under manufacturer Gm0rb, in insert effects. For a Synths folder install, run vstscanner again.

After an update, the page looks old or controls are missing

Restart MPC (it keeps plugin pages in memory), then remove and re-insert the plugin on your tracks.

My IRs don't appear

Check they're .wav files in a folder named reverbs, no more than four folders deep on its drive; switch the list to MY IRS and tap RESCAN. In MPC's own preset menu, new IRs show up the next time the plugin loads.

My knobs jumped back when I picked an IR

Picking one of your own IRs starts it from Init's settings (chapter 5). Factory IRs keep your knobs. To keep a sound with your own IR, save it as a My Preset.

My phone can't open the page

  • The phone must be on the same Wi-Fi network as the MPC (not mobile data, not a guest network that isolates devices).
  • Type http:// and the exact address the status line shows, port included.
  • If it says "Page closed", tap BROWSE again. (For TONE3000 downloads, the MPC itself also needs internet access.)

The reverb is too loud, boomy or harsh

Lower Mix first; then raise Low Cut (boom) or lower High Cut (harshness). High Feedback builds up level by design: lower it, or lower Mix. On drums and vocals, Duck clears space without lowering the tail.

Moving trim, fade or time knobs doesn't change the sound immediately

Expected: the IR is reshaped in the background and crossfaded in. Long IRs with Decay above 100% take longest, especially with LONG IR on.

Reporting a problem

Open an issue at github.com/gmorb/mpc-vst-liminal-hz/issues with your device, MPC OS version and what you did. Akai doesn't support third-party plugins, so please don't contact them about it.

Appendix B

Glossary

The terms used in this manual.

ConvolutionPlaying a sound through an impulse response: every sample of the input triggers a scaled copy of the whole IR, and all the copies are added up.
Impulse response (IR)A recording of how a space or device responds to a single, instantaneous sound. It captures the space's whole character.
Dry / wetDry is your original sound; wet is the reverb. Mix sets the balance.
Pre-delayThe gap between the dry sound and the start of the reverb.
Note valueA length of time in beats at the project tempo: 1/4 is a quarter note, 1/16 a sixteenth. T (triplet) is two thirds of it; D (dotted) is one and a half times it.
DuckingTurning one signal down while another plays. Duck turns the reverb down while your input plays.
Early reflectionsThe first distinct echoes from the nearest walls, in the first tens of milliseconds. Start trims them away.
TailThe diffuse, decaying part of a reverb after the early reflections.
RT60 / decay timeThe time a reverb takes to fade by 60 dB. The sonics line's "decay 2.8 s" is this figure.
DiffuseA smooth wash of reflections with no distinct repeats, as opposed to echoes.
Third-octave bandA slice of the frequency range one third of an octave wide. Decay analyses and reshapes the IR band by band.
ResamplingConverting audio from one sample rate to another. Used when an IR's rate differs from the MPC's, and for Stretch.
Alias toneAn unwanted tone created when high frequencies are resampled without filtering. Band-limited resampling (LONG IR, Gen 2) prevents them.
Mid/sideSplitting stereo into the centre (mid) and the difference (side). Width scales the side.
PackA subfolder of IRs: a factory kind, one of your folders, or one TONE3000 tone.
Q-LinkOne of the MPC's or Force's assignable hardware knobs.
LONG IRThe Gen 1 switch for 20-second IRs and Gen 2's IR preparation quality.
Gen 1 / Gen 2The two generations of MPC OS hardware: 32-bit RK3288 (Force, MPC Live, Live II, One, X, Key 61) and 64-bit RK3588 (MPC Live III and others).
External PluginsMPC OS 3.10.1's place for third-party plugins, loaded from a VST2 Plugins folder once External Plugin Support is on.
TONE3000An online library of captures and IRs shared by musicians. Liminal Hz downloads its IRs.
